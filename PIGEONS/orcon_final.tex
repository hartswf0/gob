\title{Treating Workers Like Guided Missile Pigeons:\\
Cybernetics, Behaviorism, and Symbolic Systems of Control}

\titlerunning{Treating Workers Like Guided Missile Pigeons}

\author{Watson Hartsoe}
\authorrunning{W. Hartsoe}

\institute{Digital Media, Georgia Institute of Technology\\

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industry~5.0 is officially defined by the European Commission as a paradigm
that places \emph{the wellbeing of the worker at the centre of the production
process}~\cite{breque2021industry}. This paper challenges that narrative by
tracing the genealogy of human-in-the-loop systems back to B.F. Skinner's
Project Pigeon (later Project ORCON)---a WWII initiative that integrated living
pigeons into cybernetic guidance loops via operant conditioning. We introduce
the concept of the \emph{Thick Machine}: a system whose operational efficacy is
inextricable from its cultural legitimacy. Project ORCON failed not because the
pigeons malfunctioned but because it triggered a \emph{semiotic refusal}---the
military's sociotechnical imaginary could not absorb a farm animal steering a
weapon of war. Modern systems---Amazon's ADAPT productivity tracker, Cogito's
empathy-coaching AI, and Uber's gamified driver management---succeed precisely
because they have solved this symbolic problem, disguising operant conditioning
as corporate care. Critically, the pigeon-guided bomb pioneered the conductive
translucent touchscreen interface: the same interface workers now peck at in
algorithmically monitored workplaces. We outline six conditions for
\emph{Symbolic Sovereignty}, arguing that genuine human-centric work
interaction design requires the fundamental right to remain illegible to
algorithmic overseers.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Cybernetics $\cdot$ Behaviorism $\cdot$ Symbolic Anthropology $\cdot$

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction: The Semiotic Refusal}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Project Pigeon was not a technical failure. It was a cultural scandal.

In 1943, B.F.~Skinner proposed a guidance system for the \emph{Pelican} glide
bomb. Lacking reliable radar, Skinner trained pigeons via operant conditioning
to peck at a target projected onto a conductive translucent screen inside the
missile's nose cone; a pneumatic servo link translated their pecks into steering
corrections. In simulations the birds were flawless---unjammable, structurally
redundant, and psychologically resilient. Skinner recorded individual pigeons
pecking \textbf{over~10,000~times in a~45-minute session}, and their targeting
accuracy remained battle-sharp up to six years after initial
conditioning~\cite{skinner1960pigeons,cyberneticzoo2025}.

Yet in~1944 the National Defense Research Committee cancelled the project.
Skinner observed that during his final demonstration---despite a
\emph{perfect performance}---the spectacle \emph{simply reminded the committee
of how utterly fantastic our proposal was}~\cite{skinner1960pigeons}. The
project was killed not by technical failure but by what we term a
\textbf{semiotic refusal}: the military's sociotechnical imaginary of rational,
masculine, electromechanical control could not absorb the image of a farm
animal steering a weapon of war~\cite{pigeonbombs2025}.

Seventy years later, the pigeons have returned---but the semiotic crisis has
been resolved. In the era of Industry~5.0, the worker \emph{is} the pigeon.
Through technologies like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF),
Operator Digital Twins (ODTs), and platform gamification, human workers are
integrated into cybernetic loops as organic servomechanisms. Unlike Skinner's
crude nose cone, modern systems wrap this behavioral extraction in a layer of
\emph{thick description}~\cite{geertz1973interpretation}---framing surveillance
as wellness, conditioning as collaboration, and operant feedback as empathetic
AI.

A final material irony anchors the genealogy. The conductive translucent screen
the pigeons pecked to steer the Pelican was one of the earliest functional
touch-based input interfaces ever built, predating commercial touchscreens by
decades~\cite{pigeonbombs2025,nist2025}. The worker who taps a productivity
dashboard in~2026 is, in a direct historical sense, pecking Skinner's screen.
The interface changed. The architecture did not.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Six Observations}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{The cybernetic-behaviorist synthesis treats organisms as
servomechanisms.} Internal states are irrelevant to the system's objective
function. Behavior is shaped by feedback to correct error signals. The pigeon
does not need to understand war. It needs to understand grain.

\item \textbf{RLHF operationalizes human culture as a Skinner box.} It resolves
the AI alignment problem not through ethical reasoning but by treating human
approval as a scalar reward signal---conditioning an artificial policy the way
Skinner conditioned a bird~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.

\item \textbf{The locus of extraction has shifted from labor power to semiotic
surplus.} The system extracts not only work but the worker's capacity to
interpret their own exhaustion, frustration, and disengagement. The algorithm
defines what those states \emph{mean} before the worker can.

\item \textbf{The Thick Machine succeeds where the Thin Machine failed.}
Project ORCON was rejected because the conditioning was naked and visible.
Modern AI is thick: it uses the language of empathy, engagement, and safety to
disguise the mechanical nature of the control loop.

\item \textbf{The Operator-in-the-Loop is a rhetorical illusion.} The human is
not the director of the system but its organic error-correction component for
edge cases where silicon fails. The worker believes they are training the AI.
The AI is conditioning the worker to produce cleaner data.

\item \textbf{Symbolic Sovereignty is the right to illegibility.} Genuine
human-centric design requires the right to withhold physiological and emotional
states from the feedback loop---to refuse to be a variable in the system's
optimization problem.

\end{enumerate}

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Cybernetic-Behaviorist Foundation}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Norbert Wiener's work on anti-aircraft predictors established the ontology that
underpins modern workplace analytics~\cite{wiener1948cybernetics}. To shoot
down an enemy plane, Wiener modeled the pilot as an integrated servomechanism
operating within a feedback loop. Under combat stress, he observed, human
behavior sheds complexity and defaults to predictable patterns. The pilot
becomes, functionally, a machine. Wiener coined \emph{cybernetics} from the
Greek for \emph{steersman}, emphasizing self-regulation through continuous
information flow~\cite{warpeace2025}.

Crucially, early cybernetics was \emph{profoundly behaviorist} in its founding
commitments: both fields sidestepped the unobservable interiority of the mind
and focused exclusively on observable inputs, outputs, and the feedback loops
connecting them~\cite{deeplearning2025}. Concurrently, B.F. Skinner formalized
operant conditioning as a totalizing framework for behavioral engineering: the
Skinner box engineered an environment so completely that the organism's only
meaningful degree of freedom was compliance with the reward
schedule~\cite{skinner1953science}.

When combined, cybernetics and behaviorism produce a framework in which the
biological organism is no longer a sovereign, interpretive subject. It is a
black-box variable whose outputs can be optimized if the environmental inputs,
reward schedules, and feedback loops are correctly calibrated.

Project ORCON was the physical instantiation of this synthesis. The pigeon's
eye was the sensor. Its neck muscles were the actuators. Grain was the
reinforcement signal. The servo link was the feedback mechanism. The bird did
not need to understand geopolitics. It needed to understand the loop.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Project ORCON and the Thick Machine}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Why did the military reject a weapon that worked? The answer requires Clifford
Geertz's distinction between a \emph{twitch} and a
\emph{wink}~\cite{geertz1973interpretation}. To Skinner, the pigeon's peck was
a twitch---a kinematic motion to be utilized. To the military, war was a thick
cultural system of honor, responsibility, and technological gravity. A pigeon
steering a bomb was a \emph{wink} of absurdity that threatened the dignity of
the entire institution.

We introduce the concept of the \textbf{Thick Machine}: a technological system
whose operational efficacy is inextricable from its cultural legitimacy. Project
ORCON was a failed Thick Machine---technically superior, symbolically toxic. As
historiography of the project makes clear, military administrators struggled not
only with the aesthetics of the system but with its ethical implications: birds
that had been named, trained, and bonded with handlers were being sent on
suicide missions~\cite{pigeonbombs2025,warpeace2025}. The same military that
celebrated carrier pigeons with Dickin Medals---\emph{the animals' Victoria
Cross}~\cite{warpeace2025}---could not reconcile the heroism narrative with
kamikaze deployment. The Thick Machine failed because its symbolic register was
irresolvably split.

There is further irony inscribed in the material history of ORCON. The
translucent conductive screen the pigeons pecked---which detected beak position
and translated it into steering corrections---was one of the earliest functional
touch-based input interfaces ever built, predating commercial touchscreens by
several decades~\cite{pigeonbombs2025}. The descendants of that screen are now
standard equipment in every algorithmically monitored workplace. The worker who
touches their productivity dashboard is, in a direct genealogical sense,
pecking Skinner's screen.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Industry~5.0: The Sanitized ORCON}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Industry~5.0 is officially defined by the European Commission as placing
\emph{the wellbeing of the worker at the centre of the production
process}~\cite{breque2021industry}. A critical analysis of its technical
architecture reveals a different structure: Industry~5.0 is Project ORCON with
a better public relations department.

The modern ORCON has solved Skinner's semiotic problem in three moves. First,
it does not put the worker in a nose cone---it puts them in an \emph{augmented
workspace}. Second, it does not offer grain---it offers gamified engagement
points, wellness check-ins, and real-time mental health support. Third, and
most critically, it hides the conditioning behind a user interface of care.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{RLHF: The Silicon Pigeon and the Majority Vote}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is the formalized behaviorism
of the 21st century. As Sutton and Barto establish, the Reward
Hypothesis---that all goals can be framed as the maximization of a scalar reward
signal---is the foundational axiom of modern AI
training~\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}. In RLHF, human workers label outputs,
their judgments aggregated into a reward signal that shapes the model's policy.
The structural homology with Skinner's box is not metaphorical. It is
mathematical.

What has been underappreciated is that Skinner himself had already designed a
primitive alignment mechanism. When multiple pigeons disagreed about a target,
the system implemented \emph{majority rule}: two birds would align on one
target, and the third would be \emph{punished for its minority
opinion}~\cite{deeplearning2025}. This is proto-RLHF: aggregate the human
signal, penalize outliers, train toward statistical consensus. The modern RLHF
pipeline does not merely resemble this. It formalizes it at scale across
millions of human annotators.

The recursion is the critical mechanism. RLHF workers are conditioned to
condition the machine. Their individual judgments must align with statistical
consensus to maintain quality scores. Human ethics is not imported into the
system---it is reduced to a reward function, then fed back as a behavioral
constraint. Morality becomes, in Skinner's terms, a schedule of reinforcement.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Operator Digital Twins: Capturing the Interior}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Cogito's emotional AI platform---deployed at MetLife, Humana, and five Fortune
25 companies---analyzes over~200 acoustic and lexical signals per millisecond
during live customer service calls~\cite{cxtoday2022cogito,cogitostex2025}. It
delivers real-time behavioral corrections to agents via on-screen
notifications: \emph{Speaking slowly---try increasing your pace.} A pink heart
icon: \emph{Empathy Cue---Think about how the customer is feeling. Try to
relate.}~\cite{timecogito2019}. Cogito's CEO frames this as coaching to prevent
\emph{compassion fatigue}. A critic in the same article offers a different
reading: the system constitutes \emph{intensive surveillance} supporting
\emph{a priori justification for potentially firing workers based on the
assessment of a smart machine that neither the worker nor the manager is able to
contest or validate}~\cite{timecogito2019}.

This operationalizes Geertz's thick/thin distinction in a coercive
direction~\cite{geertz1973interpretation}. The ODT interprets thin biometric
signals as thick inner states: heart rate variability becomes engagement; vocal
prosody becomes emotional stability. The system performs cultural
interpretation---it translates the wink---but does so unilaterally, without
the worker's input, and purely in service of the production objective.

Haraway's framework of co-constitutive \emph{naturecultures} is relevant here,
but in a perverse form~\cite{haraway2003companion}. The worker and the ODT are
co-constitutive, but not mutually so: the algorithm defines what the worker's
states mean, and the worker has no reciprocal claim on the algorithm's
interpretation. This is what Kellogg et al.\ term \emph{algorithmic
control}---the restriction of human autonomy through systems that read
biological signals and intervene automatically~\cite{kellogg2020algorithms}.
The worker's body is treated as a machine component requiring predictive
maintenance to prevent production downtime.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Amazon ADAPT: The Feedback Loop Made Flesh}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Amazon's Associate Development and Performance Tracker (ADAPT) makes the
cybernetic logic explicit. The system tracks warehouse workers via
radio-frequency handheld scanners, logging every minute of ``Time Off Task''
(TOT): bathroom visits, conversations with colleagues, navigational errors. A
worker accumulating 120~minutes of TOT in a single day is automatically
terminated, without manager input~\cite{vice2022amazon}. Internal documents
filed with the National Labor Relations Board show managers required each shift
to identify the single ``top offender'' per manager, interrogate that worker
about each unproductive timestamp, and provide disciplinary coaching: \emph{AA
stated he was in the bathroom for the 10~TOT. Coached AA on the TOT
policy.}~\cite{vice2022amazon}

Workers with irritable bowel syndrome report receiving write-ups for
\emph{unaccounted for idle time}~\cite{timeamazon2023}. The biological
interiority of the organism---the body's own rhythms of illness, fatigue, and
digestion---is classified as a performance deviation. The system does not
distinguish between a malfunction in the warehouse's conveyor system and a
malfunction in the worker's gastrointestinal tract. Both are TOT.

Washington State's Department of Labor formally linked Amazon's monitoring
regime to above-industry injury rates: the expectation that workers \emph{maintain
a very high pace of work or face discipline is directly tied to the incidence of
injuries}~\cite{seattletimes2021amazon}. Amazon rebranded this finding as a
commitment to becoming \emph{Earth's Safest Place to Work}. This is the Thick
Machine's cultural legitimacy mechanism in operation.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Uber: The Gamified Skinner Box}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Uber's 2018 driver app redesign deployed gamification to resolve the platform's
labor supply problem without changing driver compensation~\cite{uber2022gig}.
Heat-map surge zones rendered in deep red, growing line-graph icons, and
notifications about \emph{high demand areas} deploy color, motion, and visual
urgency to trigger driver movement---while Uber's founder simultaneously
declared: \emph{We are not setting the price. The market is setting the
price}~\cite{ubercasestudy2016}. The conditioning is operational; its existence
is institutionally denied. Drivers on public forums describe the system as
\emph{manipulative games}, \emph{psychological ploys}, and \emph{social
engineering}~\cite{uber2022gig}.

Vasudevan and Chan's~\cite{vasudevan2022gamification} empirical analysis of
driver resistance identifies \emph{oppositional play modes}---``Mario Karting,''
``longhauling,'' ``shuffling''---in which drivers deploy the app's own
interactive affordances in unintended ways to protect their labor supply. This
is the Right to Friction in practice: spontaneous, worker-generated semiotic
resistance to seamless integration into the ORCON feedback loop.

\section{The Semiotic Paradox of the Pigeon-Dove Split}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

The military rejected Skinner's pigeons because they were too
\emph{biological}---too alive, too morally encumbered. This tension reappears in
the pigeon-dove split documented in the cultural literature on
Columbidae~\cite{warpeace2025}. Scientifically, pigeon and dove are the same
organism---members of Columbidae, distinguished only by size and color
convention. Yet culturally, \emph{dove} carries connotations of purity, peace,
and spiritual elevation, while \emph{pigeon} connotes nuisance, contamination,
and expendability. This linguistic bifurcation is, as the cultural literature
describes it, a form of \emph{semiotic engineering}: human societies split the
symbol to manage contradictory meanings attributed to the same entity.

The contemporary ORCON performs an identical operation on the worker. The
productive, compliant, biometrically optimal worker is a dove---\emph{engaged},
\emph{resilient}, \emph{human-centric}. The worker who flags as stressed,
resistant, or biometrically anomalous is a pigeon---an error state to be
corrected, a productivity risk to be managed. The algorithm performs the split.
The worker has no input into which category they occupy today.

Amazon's rebranding of ADAPT's injury-producing surveillance regime as a
wellness initiative; Cogito's positioning of acoustic behavioral analysis as
\emph{compassion fatigue prevention}; Uber's construction of exploitative
gamification as \emph{helping drivers earn more}---these are not
misrepresentations. They are the Thick Machine functioning correctly. The
cultural layer is not a mask placed over the control loop. It \emph{is} the
control loop.

\section{Six Conditions for Symbolic Sovereignty}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Symbolic sovereignty is the capacity of an individual to control the production
of meaning regarding their own existence---to decide what constitutes a twitch
and what constitutes a wink. The systematic deployment of ODTs, RLHF, and
algorithmic management transfers this capacity from human subjects to algorithmic
overseers. Resistance must therefore be semiotic. We propose six conditions,
each corresponding to one of the six observations above.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{The Right to Illegibility.} Workers must have the operational
capability to selectively blind the system to their biometric and emotional
states without punitive consequence. A privacy mode for the nervous system.
California's AB-701~\cite{ab701california2022}, which gives workers the right
to request three months of their own productivity data, is a first legislative
instantiation of this right.

\item \textbf{The Right to Semantic Autonomy.} The meaning of biometric and
behavioral signals cannot be dictated unilaterally by the algorithm. Workers
must have the capacity to contest and override the system's interpretations of
their own states. The Whittaker critique of Cogito---that neither worker nor
manager can contest an algorithmic assessment---defines precisely the condition
this right is designed to counter~\cite{timecogito2019}.

\item \textbf{The Right to Interiority.} The extraction of semiotic surplus
value---using a worker's emotional reactions to train a model, using their
bodily outputs to calibrate a productivity algorithm---must be explicitly
consensual and compensated as labor.

\item \textbf{The Right to Agency.} The human must remain the steersman
(\emph{cybernetes}), not the steered. The system must offer recommendations,
not automated interventions that override human judgment without appeal. ADAPT's
automated termination---firing a worker without manager input---is the exact
negation of this right.

\item \textbf{The Right to Friction.} Resistance to seamless human-machine
integration is not malfunction---it is evidence of life. The ``oppositional play
modes'' documented by Vasudevan and Chan among Uber
drivers~\cite{vasudevan2022gamification} are not system errors. They are the
Right to Friction exercised under conditions of asymmetric power.

\item \textbf{The Right to Interpretation.} Final authority over the meaning of
one's own existence must remain with the individual and their community, not
the algorithmic overseer. Flourishing is not a KPI. The UK REF system---which
has documented \emph{goal displacement} effects, reshaping academic behavior
toward metric optimization at the expense of genuine knowledge production---is
an ORCON deployment in the cognitive labor domain~\cite{ref2024metrics}.

\end{enumerate}

Second-order cybernetics offers a theoretical foundation for this
framework~\cite{deeplearning2025}. Where first-order cybernetics treats the
organism as a component observed from outside the loop, second-order cybernetics
insists that the observer is inside the system, and that being observed changes
behavior. The ORCON depends on this: the worker who knows they are being
monitored adjusts their behavior not toward authentic flourishing but toward the
system's model of what flourishing looks like. The pigeon did not know it was
being conditioned. The worker does---and that second-order awareness is itself
recursively incorporated into the conditioning architecture.

Symbolic sovereignty is the capacity to introduce a gap in this loop---to
perform compliance while preserving interiority. It is the right to wink while
the system reads a twitch.

%%-------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion: The Architecture Endures}
%%-------------------------------------------------------------------

Project Pigeon was dismissed as a crackpot idea. It was actually the blueprint.

Skinner proved that if you control the environment, the inputs, and the reward
schedule, you can make a pigeon steer a missile. Industry~5.0 proves that if
you add a layer of digital abstraction and a language of care, you can make a
human do the same---while the human thanks the system for monitoring their
mental health.

The Thick Machine framework clarifies what changed and what did not. The
semiotic refusal of~1944 was a one-time problem: once the biological body is
hidden inside a dashboard, once the conditioning is rebranded as collaboration,
once the grain is called wellness, the cultural barrier dissolves. Amazon ADAPT,
Cogito Dialog, and Uber's gamified driver app are not analogous to Project
ORCON. They \emph{are} Project ORCON, upgraded to achieve the cultural
legitimacy that Skinner's pigeons could never provide.

The touchscreen genealogy is the final, unsettling closure of this argument.
The conductive screen the pigeon pecked to steer the Pelican is the direct
conceptual ancestor of the touchscreen the warehouse worker, call-center agent,
and gig driver peck to log their engagement~\cite{pigeonbombs2025}. The
interface changed. The loop did not.

The only question Industry~5.0 has not yet answered---and the only one that
matters for human work interaction design---is whether the organism inside the
nose cone can learn to peck somewhere else.
