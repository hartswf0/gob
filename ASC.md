# THE_FINAL_TYPESCRIPT

## 1.0 Architecture
### 1.1
The EHR failed not because it is slow, but because it is passive. A passive ledger converts clinical expertise into clerical throughput.
### 1.2
"Click reduction" optimizes captivity. It accelerates the same misallocation of human attention.
### 1.3
The strategic move is a phase transition: system of record to system of intelligence.

## 2.0 Labor Reallocation
### 2.1
The clinic requires a digital autonomic layer: background execution for background work.
### 2.2
Administrative respiration must be machine-native: documentation capture, data reconciliation, order staging, and message routing.
### 2.3
Clinician labor must be reserved for diagnosis, judgment, and irreversible decisions.

## 3.0 Agent Doctrine
### 3.1
An intelligence stack is valid only when each agent has a narrow mandate and a visible handoff.
### 3.2
Scribe agent: converts encounter speech into structured draft memory.
### 3.3
Aggregation agent: fuses fragmented records into reviewable, low-risk proposals.
### 3.4
Command agent: translates spoken intent into staged orders, referrals, and codes.
### 3.5
Gatekeeper agent: resolves routine patient intent and escalates emergency language without negotiation.

## 4.0 Control Law
### 4.1
Execution pattern is fixed: speak intent, inspect staging, sign commitment.
### 4.2
Automation without veto is coercion; assistance without accountability is theater.
### 4.3
Safety is not a feature layer. Safety is the routing law that halts routine flow when risk language appears.
### 4.4
When machine staging hardens into institutional action, authorship and liability must remain human-legible at every step.

## 5.0 Strategic Axioms
### 5.1
Do not optimize clerical burden; abolish it at the architectural level.
### 5.2
Do not deploy general intelligence; deploy constrained agents with explicit scopes.
### 5.3
Do not measure speed first; measure decision integrity under speed.
### 5.4
Do not hide the chain of action; expose provenance from utterance to signed order.
